# Direct a film from its source

The Figma iterations established a reference for care: recognizable source artwork,
purposeful motion, varied scale, short punctuation beats, readable holds and a simple
finish. Reuse that process for each brand. Copying its red frame, ribbons or techno
onto unrelated products does not reproduce the quality.

## Choose what deserves to move

Read the source sheet before approving the cut. Favor product artwork, meaningful
interface states and distinctive details over navigation, testimonials and generic
cards. Keep the site's observed type and palette unless the brief requests a departure.
Fonts and colors are observed from the site, not certified against a brand manual.
Use relevant documentation to understand product actions without inventing features.

Auto mode tries native source components first. Native availability is not a ranking
of artistic value: choose hybrid if the strongest story lives in published artwork.
Retained DOM and SVG can move as named layers; embedded images remain flat. Raster
bands can assemble an image, but its internal controls do not become editable UI.

## Give each shot a job

Choose wide staging for a broad interface and side staging for tall artwork. Change
scale and direction when the product action calls for it. Macro reveals detail before
context, assemble brings parts together, and shuttle moves the source into position.
Keep arrivals short enough to leave a readable complete-source hold. Native macro can
draw retained SVG paths; raster macro only reframes the captured image. Use accent
beats sparingly and end with the brand alone on dark.

Plan a relationship between neighboring shots: a shared shape, position, subject or
direction. The `action` and `handoff` fields record these notes. They do not implement
new behavior. A connecting line that becomes a cursor, a brush that paints a letter,
or an object traveling between scenes requires additional authored motion. Do not
describe those effects as delivered without inspecting them in the export.

## Score the action

Choose a sonic character for the specific story. Built-in profiles synthesize an
original procedural score with cuts and source arrival cues on the same clock. They
do not reproduce the recorded-drum production of the custom Figma soundtrack.
For that level of production, create or obtain an appropriate finished score and use
`rescore` with `audioFile`. Keep voiceover and effects; name imported music tracks to
replace explicitly. Listen through the whole film, including the final release.

## Review before delivery

Inspect exported frames during arrival, action and the settled hold, then play the
film. Check source relevance, legible typography, clipping, repeated staging, coherent
handoffs, intentional pauses and musical accents. Compare against the approved film
for these specific qualities. Preserve that film when making a new take.

Repair observed weaknesses through the shared pipeline when they recur. For an
individual bespoke transition, refine the authored motion and verify its geometry at
several times. CSS individual scale/rotate on a motion-path element can transform its
path coordinates; check pointer/object alignment rather than trusting the path data.

The returned `quality` report counts sources and staging and flags repetition. It is
advisory. A structurally valid export is not artistic approval. If a review fails or
cannot be performed, say so. The current finite choreography vocabulary improves the
starting point; it does not guarantee the custom Figma film's quality for most prompts.
